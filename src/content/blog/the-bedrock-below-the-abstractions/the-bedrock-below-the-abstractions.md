# The Bedrock
[Ryan Fleury](https://x.com/rfleury) recently wrote in his [tweet](https://x.com/rfleury/status/2076789388760195355) something that really resonated with me.

> "And in fact, every abstraction layer leaks to some degree, which means there is always value in understanding the full stack. We’ve seen what happens when programmers ignore this truth, and it’s not good."

As the way we write and read code becomes more abstracted year by year, it is once again more important than ever to understand the building blocks that these abstractions are built upon.

This sentiment is something that everyone in the programming spheres has heard over the years. I am not sure when this started _(probably when we moved away from assembly as the main way to write software)_, but when I first entered the field in 2022, there was a large group of people talking about how, instead of learning every web framework, the programmer should understand what these frameworks are built upon, what they do under the hood, and why they exist in the first place. Turned out these people were right.

Now, once again, we have moved one abstraction further, and people are using LLMs to write software in a [**black box**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box) where the quality of the software is solely evaluated by the artefacts it produces. In a way, I don't think the step has even been that radical when you think about how people have learned to rely on libraries and packages built by other people.

If LLMs are just the next layer, then the old lesson about understanding the building blocks applies to them exactly as it did to frameworks or other libraries.

# My plan

Whatever the future may hold in the field of software development, time and time again we have been shown that these fundamental building blocks of computer science are what allow people to understand the new layers of abstraction the fastest and easily transfer between them.

As someone who entered the field by learning to program web applications at work and kinda skipped these fundamentals, my plan for the future is to bet on these fundamentals and deepen my knowledge in that area.

And that is what this blog site will be based on. In the style of [Shawn Wang](https://swyx.io/), this blog will be me [learning in public](https://swyx.io/learn-in-public) and writing about what I learn, discover, and think.
